# Soviet and Warsaw Pact target material: search of November 2026

Search date 2026-11-01, about 10:04 to 10:36 UTC. Only what was actually opened is reported. After a second read (`second_read_2026-11.md`), 44 rows were entered in `data/curated/labels/wp_1977_gsa_lessons.csv` and 2 in `wp_1975_soyuz75.csv`. No other candidate row below has been entered, and those rows still need checking by a second reader. The downloads of this search were in `/var/tmp/soviet_search/`, which no longer exists; hashes of the documents behind the entered rows are under "Hashes" below.

## Result in brief

Eleven documents that name targets are new to the repo. 97 candidate rows follow.

The largest find is not a lead from the owner's instructions. It is the rest of the CIA set of 1977 General Staff Academy lessons, which the repo had sampled through one lesson. Five more lessons give real place names for airfields, nuclear warhead depots, Pershing and Lance areas and warning posts, mostly in the FRG with some in the Netherlands. The lesson already held lists 14 airfields by number; these lessons name them.

The second find is warplans.org, which hosts a large scan of the 1970 Coastal Front map and a copy of the 1979 exercise map. The 1970 scan resolves open issue 4 in `data/notes/warsaw_pact.md`: all 17 bomb symbols can now be placed, against 5 named before. The 1979 map is a real primary image, but this copy is too coarse to read its targets.

No war plan naming new targets was found. Everything new except the 1970 map is an exercise, a lesson or a secondary account. No Bulgarian, Czechoslovak 1977/1989, or Lautsch 2014 target list was obtained.

### Caveats that matter for use

The lessons are one fictional scenario, so 5 documents do not make 5 independent lists. The sides are colour-coded, but the places and many of the NATO units are real. Treat them as one source, an extension of `wp_1977_gsa_front_lesson.csv`. Rows from later lessons are follow-up strikes on 12 and 13 September, not the initial strike, and the notes say so. Some repeat a place under a different class.

The lessons disagree slightly among themselves. Lesson 2 (p.15) lists airfield No. 29 (Bremen) and has an OCR variant 35 for Kloppenburg. The nuclear planning lesson already held (0001197534, p.8) lists numbers 07, 16, 12, 23, 24, 27, 28, 30, 31, 33, 34, 41, 43, 46, so 24 where Lesson 2 has 29. I did not resolve this.

Most lesson text was read from archive.org OCR. I compared the scan for 0001197557 pp.13-14 and 0001197561 p.27 only. OCR did garble at least one reading (LATEN on p.27), so every quote needs checking against the scan. Page numbers were confirmed from the OCR page objects; PDF page equals printed page in these files.

The 1970 map rows are read by eye from symbols beside printed base-map labels. A symbol has no text of its own, so these rows are less firm than a typed list. The count does support the reading: 12 symbols were found outside Zealand, and with the 5 on Zealand that gives the 17 Nielsen et al. 2016 state. Nielsen's description ("the large ports along the German and Dutch coastlines") is loose, since Apeldoorn and Utrecht are inland and Antwerp is Belgian. Inset 6 is handwritten and at the limit of the scan, so several names are uncertain and one is undeciphered.

warplans.org captions the 1970 map as a "Plan of an offensive operation of the Coastal Front troops, from a February 1970 Staff exercise". The sheet itself is titled a plan and is approved by the minister, and the repo already files it as a plan. This conflict is not resolved. It should be settled before the 22 rows are used as plan evidence.

The 1979 map bears "D18" and "25.5" in its title. The press called the exercise "Seven Days to the River Rhine", and the fleet orders from what Wilson Center lists as a 6-13 June 1979 exercise are dated differently. I could not confirm that these are the same exercise, nor that "Seven Days" is its title on any document opened.

The Guardian's list of cities for 1979 differs from the "known targets" in `wp_1979_seven_days.csv`, which trace to other plans. It is the first list opened that describes the 1979 map directly, but it is a reporter's selection. It should stay at audit level until a better scan allows checking.

Uhl and Ivkin state that "one can only speculate as to the possible targets" and then that it "is also certain" missiles were aimed at the named population centres. They also say Paris and London fell outside R-5M range after the move to Kaliningrad. Der Spiegel and Uhl disagree on the Thor counties (Norfolk and Lincolnshire against Yorkshire and Suffolk). These rows support only the audit status of the existing R-5M row.
