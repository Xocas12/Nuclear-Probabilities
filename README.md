# nuclear-probabilities

What made a place a nuclear target, as far as declassified lists can show? And given a stated stockpile,
how likely is it that an area is struck? This is historical analysis on public sources. Towns are described
as they stood at a document's date and labelled from target lists of US and Warsaw Pact documents, 1945-1977.
Three of those lists are registered as war plans, all Warsaw Pact. Only one, Czechoslovak, was read at source, and it links no
designated town; the two Polish ones are known from a scholar's table and paraphrase of files not read. The largest list, the US Strategic Air
Command's (SAC) 1956 study, is a requirements study (an estimate of the weapons needed against a list of targets,
not an order to strike them), and the searches recorded in `data/notes/` found no declassified Soviet war plan naming places. The results are in
**[REPORT.md](REPORT.md)**. [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) is the original design;
[PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md) is the dated work log, kept as history.

## What was found

PR-AUC, the area under the precision-recall curve, measures how many of a model's top-ranked towns are on a
list. A list's base rate is what no information scores: 0.49 for SAC, 0.07 for the other lists.
Where a list is incomplete a town off it is unlabeled, so PR-AUC understates precision there. Each interval is 95%, the first
resampling towns and "by blocks" resampling 300 km squares; a difference counts only if both exclude zero.
A **scope** is the set of lists a model is fitted on: two global scopes (every attacker's documents, or plan documents
only), a pooled Warsaw Pact scope, and SAC's 1956 study alone. A **linked positive** is a listed place matched to a town
of the place universe. The **population rule** ranks towns by population alone; **population plus country** adds a
term for each target country.

## Data

- **Labels.** The 26 lists registered in `data/curated/labels/PLANS.yaml`, each row quoting its page. `PLANS.yaml` keeps four things apart for
  every list: the attacker, the date, the document type (war plan, target selection, requirements study, policy study, exercise, war game,
  staff-academy lesson, written `training` in the tables, civil-defence list, a mirror: one side's guess at the other's
  targets, a document disputed between plan and exercise, or a reconstruction by journalists, which is excluded) and an
  evidence grade.
  Only types the protocol counts as plans count towards a state's model; exercises and lessons are labelled as such
  wherever they are used. Soviet and Warsaw Pact evidence is thin: no Soviet war plan naming places was found, and the
  three registered Warsaw Pact war plans give 6 linked positives between them, none from a plan read at source (REPORT,
  "What the evidence cannot support"). Two Warsaw Pact link errors were corrected before the committed run (`configs/protocol_amendment_17.yaml`):
  Lato-67's German offset rows now credit four towns, and two Burza rows with a direction but no distance no longer
  credit Hamburg and Schleswig. What that changed is in REPORT, "Changed by the corrected Warsaw Pact links".
- **Places and features.** Towns, mostly of 10,000 or more, from censuses as near each document's date as could be found, with
  population, rank, distances to coast, capital and other towns, and, where a source exists for that date, military
  sites, air and missile defences and the Correlates of War project's (COW) National Material Capabilities. A feature should not postdate its
  document, but some do. Railways, ports and airfields taken from today's data are flagged and the results are repeated without them;
  other late inputs are not flagged and stay in those repeats: the 1959 Soviet census in every population column of the 1956
  list, Hungary's and the Czech lands' towns interpolated with 1960 and 1961 censuses, East Germany's and Poland's with
  end-1956 figures and Slovakia's with 1958 ones, Kurchatov's 1990 figure, North Korean, North Vietnamese and Mongolian
  towns chosen by their size today, and undated West European sites counted for 1961 and 1964 (REPORT, "Inputs later than the document"). Only a separate USSR rerun on the 1939 census avoids the 1959 census.
- **Stockpiles.** `data/curated/stockpiles/`: inventories by state and year from the Federation of
  American Scientists (FAS), its Status of World Nuclear Forces table of 2026, the Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC)
  and Our World in Data (OWID), a series citing FAS, and 2026 shares by delivery, rounded, from the Stockholm
  International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI). The Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists' Nuclear Notebook was not read:
  its site's robots.txt disallows this project's agent (`data/curated/stockpiles/README.md`). Every other assumption is
  stated beside its source or marked as having none.
  The historical scenarios' reliability and losses to defences are a stand-in: US government estimates of 1953-55 for Soviet bombers
  attacking the US (Foreign Relations of the United States, FRUS), applied to US bombers in 1950 and 1956 and to Soviet
  forces in 1965 because no opened source covers those.
- **Provenance.** `data/sources.yaml`: every source's URL, licence, retrieval date and sha256. `data/ISSUES.md`
  answers the repository's issues and pull requests.

## Method

The analysis was fixed in `configs/protocol.yaml` before the final models were fitted, and changed since only by
dated amendments, four inside `configs/protocol.yaml` and `configs/protocol_amendment_5.yaml` to `configs/protocol_amendment_18.yaml`, each saying what had
already been read (one line each in `configs/HISTORY.md`). Results had been read before most of them, so the analysis
is not blind except on the sealed set.

A town off an incomplete list is treated as unlabeled, not as a negative (positive-unlabeled: each of 50 bagged fits
keeps all positives and draws as many unlabeled towns). Every model is compared with two baselines on the same towns:
ranking by population (the "population rule") and population plus one intercept per country. Holdouts are spatial
blocks of 300 km, leaving out one document at a time, and fitting on documents up to 1956 to score later ones, and the reverse. PR-AUC
is the primary metric, with paired bootstrap intervals over towns and over blocks, and calibration is reported. A
state gets its own model only if it passes the gate in the protocol (two independent plans, 50 positives, 15 outside
its largest plan); every state fails it.

The **sealed set** is the towns of the blocks in `data/curated/splits/sealed_blocks.csv`. No model was fitted on
them, and they were scored once, after the models were final. Some had been scored by the two test models run before this classifier (the M1 slice and M2 check) before
the set was drawn; the places no committed run had scored are the **blind places**, the one blind test
(`data/curated/splits/README.md`). That subset was defined after the reading of all sealed places had been seen
(`configs/protocol_amendment_12.yaml`, which refits nothing).

The map takes the model the protocol's rule selects, draws stockpiles and models together (an attacker's inventory
times its stated **theatre share**, the part assumed aimed at the scenario's countries, times reliability and
survival of defences), and shares each scenario's delivered warheads among towns in proportion to the score,
summarised on a 0.5-degree grid (1 degree for modern scenarios).

## What the evidence cannot support

- **A model of any one state's plans.** Each state is refused by the gate; the Warsaw Pact model pools exercises.
- **Prediction for a document not in the data.** No model beat population on a left-out document.
- **A blind gain over population plus country.** On the only blind places, no model beat it (REPORT, "The sealed set on blind places").
- **A pooled gain as more than each document's base rate.** The columns name a row's document almost every time
  (REPORT, "What the gains rest on"), so a model fitted on pooled lists can learn which list a row comes from.
- **Causes.** These are associations between a town's traits and its presence on a list.
- **What planners knew.** Features record what existed according to later sources.
- **Any present-day plan.** Modern map rows apply lists of 1945-1977 to towns of today: extrapolation, with no
  modern label to test it.
- **The map's probabilities as forecasts.** They follow from stated assumptions; the theatre shares have no source.

## Layout

- `data/curated/labels/`: target lists, the registry `PLANS.yaml` (attacker, date, type, grade), `SECTORS.yaml`
  (the area each list covers; a town outside it is not a negative of that list).
- `data/curated/stockpiles/`: cited inventories and assumptions. `data/sources.yaml` and `data/sources_amendment_5.yaml`: every source's provenance;
  `data/curated/features/cited_pages.csv`: the pages cited row by row in the site tables.
- `configs/protocol.yaml` and `configs/protocol_amendment_5.yaml` to `configs/protocol_amendment_18.yaml`: the analysis and its amendments. `configs/scenarios.yaml`: scenarios.
- `runs/classifier/`, `runs/probmap/`: committed outputs. `REPORT.md` is generated from them.
- `runs/m1-slice/`, `runs/m2-check/`: two early checks (M1: Soviet towns only, population and administrative features; M2: the first full 1956 feature table over the whole bloc; PROGRESS.md), on the labels of their date. They were not
  rerun after later label corrections and feed no result above. `runs/m4-transfer-check/`: the transfer check (does a model fitted on SAC's 1956 bloc towns rank the targets of the smaller lists?), rerun on the corrected labels; it reads whole countries, not sectors, and feeds no result above.

## Running it

Requires [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/) and GNU make.

```
make setup
make data       # sources of data/sources.yaml into data/raw/; see "Not all rebuildable" below
make places labels features dataset
make transfer evidence qa
make classifier # table, models, repeats and audits; several hours on 3 cores
make probmap    # about half an hour
make report     # REPORT.md, TABLES.md and README's results, from both runs
make viewer     # one self-contained page: runs/probmap/viewer/index.html
make test lint
```

`make test` alone is a partial run on a fresh checkout: the tests that compare committed outputs with tables built
from `data/raw/` (git-ignored) skip when those tables are absent, and the run lists them at its end. `make test-full`
builds the tables (`places` to `qa` and the classifier table; no model is fitted) and then counts any skip as a
failure; it needs `make data` first and a full, not shallow, clone.

`make us1985` repeats a separate check on US counties against FEMA's 1987 risk bands, `runs/m4-us-check/`, which
tests that those features join and is no part of the results above (`data/FEATURES_US.md`). Its table of US military
sites in 1985 is not in the tree, since most of those sites are still in use; the target reads it back from the commit
that added it and checks its sha256. The tree does keep that table's row specification, `scripts/us_military_1985_spec.py`:
each site's name, 1985 role, unit, town and cited page, with one coordinate (Savannah River Site); the
builder beside it would take the others from those pages. It lists features as of 1985, not targets, and ranks nothing. A shallow clone cannot run it. The pinned record is not overwritten: the rerun
goes to `runs/m4-us-check-rerun/`, whose `comparison.csv` sets each metric beside the pinned one. 21 of the 36 rows
have the same PR-AUC and 18 the same value on every metric; the largest PR-AUC difference is 0.0023, all in rows with OurAirports' airfield count, a live list fetched
again on 1 November after the record was made.

`make viewer` builds from the committed outputs alone. A clean clone's tests run without data; those that need
built tables skip.

**Not all rebuildable.** COW's data is not committed (no licence stated), so rebuilding needs its download. SIPRI's
tables are not redistributed (its terms), so `data/curated/stockpiles/delivery_mix.csv` holds rounded shares only.
`data/curated/features/bloc_air_defence_1956.csv` was made by hand from cited pages
(`scripts/bloc_air_defence_1956/README.md`). **Robots.** `make data` asks each host's robots.txt before every download and reports a refused source as not
obtained; `python -m nucprob.sources.robots` records the answer of the day for every source in `runs/qa/robots.csv`, judged on the URL the
held bytes came from (the `obtained_from` field in `data/sources.yaml` names it where that is a mirror or an archived capture). No held file came
from a refused path. Great Britain's 1981 district totals, fetched earlier from `www.nomisweb.co.uk` through a disallowed
path, were withdrawn with everything built from them (`configs/protocol_amendment_10.yaml`). Earlier transcriptions read `en.wikipedia.org` through
disallowed paths; those copies are not held, and the pages are registered through allowed ones in
`data/curated/features/cited_pages.csv`. `www.bbc.co.uk` refuses this project's agent: the one BBC page cited (`plandoc_bbc_2012_r5m`, behind the excluded list `wp_1959_r5m_operation_atom`) is registered but not fetched or held. Sources that could not be obtained are listed with `held` in `data/sources.yaml`.
