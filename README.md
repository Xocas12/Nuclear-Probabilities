# nuclear-probabilities

What made a place a nuclear target, as far as declassified lists can show? And given a stated stockpile,
how likely is it that an area is struck? This is historical analysis on public sources. Towns are described
as they stood at a document's date and labelled from target lists of US and Warsaw Pact documents, 1945-1977.
Three of those lists are registered as war plans, all Warsaw Pact. Only one, Czechoslovak, was read at source, and it links no
designated town; the two Polish ones are known from a scholar's table and paraphrase of files not read. The largest list, the US Strategic Air
Command's (SAC) 1956 study, is a requirements study, and the searches recorded in `data/notes/` found no declassified Soviet war plan naming places. The results are in
**[REPORT.md](REPORT.md)**. [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) is the original design.

## What was found

<!-- results: generated by python -m nucprob.classifier.report -->
PR-AUC, the area under the precision-recall curve, measures how many of a model's top-ranked towns are on a
list. A list's base rate is what no information scores: 0.49 for SAC, 0.07 for the other lists.
Where a list is incomplete a town off it is unlabeled, so PR-AUC understates precision there. Each interval is 95%, the first
resampling towns and "by blocks" resampling 300 km squares; a difference counts only if both exclude zero.

- **Inside SAC's list**, under spatial cross-validation, the selected model beats population plus a country term by
  +0.02 [+0.01, +0.03], by blocks [+0.01, +0.04]: both intervals exclude zero. Without the columns flagged as later data, with or without the defence tables, it is +0.01 [+0.01, +0.02], by blocks [-0.002, +0.03]: at least one interval reaches zero, so it cannot be told from no difference. Those repeats still use the 1959 Soviet census and other late inputs
  (REPORT, "Inputs later than the document"). The analysis is not blind: earlier results had been read (REPORT, Method).
- **On a document the model has not seen**, the richer model against ranking towns by population is -0.13 [-0.15, -0.11],
  by blocks [-0.17, -0.08] over all lists, which SAC dominates. On lists other than SAC it is +0.02 [-0.03, +0.07], by blocks [-0.08, +0.07]: at least one interval reaches zero, so it cannot be told from no difference.
  So no model has been shown to do better than population on an unseen document.
- **No state gets its own model.** The US has 1299 linked positives, but only 7 outside one document and the
  pre-committed gate asks for 15. The USSR has 0 positives in a plan document, and 50 town-list pairs from staff-academy lessons.
  A pooled Warsaw Pact model is allowed and is always labelled exercise-based. Refusals: `runs/classifier/state_gate.csv`.
- **Economic and political data** (regime, defence pacts, GDP per capita, Soviet defence plants), read apart under an
  amendment committed before fitting: on a left-out document 0 of the 6 rows on lists other
  than SAC gain, and the highest model over all lists scores 0.66 against 0.71 for the population rule. Plant counts add
  +0.001 [-0.01, +0.01] on Soviet towns.
- **The map.** Because of that transfer result the selection rule chose population as the score. The map follows people far less than in proportion: the
  1956 score moves only 6.9-fold over a 620-fold range of population, so a cell's probability also depends on how many
  towns it holds. Delivered warheads drive most of its intervals, and those rest on cited inventories and on theatre shares with no source. Modern scenarios use Natural Earth's map-label towns and are
  extrapolation: a classifier told them from the training towns at ROC-AUC 0.97 on ten features, where 0.5 would mean no shift, with
  18% outside the training's 1st-99th percentile on the one feature the score reads, and no modern label exists to test them.

## Layout

- `data/curated/labels/`: target lists, the registry `PLANS.yaml` (attacker, date, type, grade), `SECTORS.yaml`.
- `data/curated/stockpiles/`: cited inventories and assumptions. `data/sources.yaml` and `data/sources_amendment_5.yaml`: every source's provenance;
  `data/curated/features/cited_pages.csv`: the pages cited row by row in the site tables.
- `configs/protocol.yaml`: pre-committed analysis and its dated amendments, with `configs/protocol_amendment_5.yaml`,
  `configs/protocol_amendment_6.yaml`, `configs/protocol_amendment_7.yaml` and `configs/protocol_amendment_8.yaml`. `configs/scenarios.yaml`: scenarios.
- `runs/classifier/`, `runs/probmap/`: committed outputs. `REPORT.md` is generated from them.
- `runs/m1-slice/`, `runs/m2-check/`: earlier milestone checks, on the labels of their date. They were not
  rerun after later label corrections and feed no result above. `runs/m4-transfer-check/`: the transfer check, rerun on the corrected labels; it reads whole countries, not sectors, and feeds no result above.

## Running it

Requires [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/) and GNU make.

```
make setup
make data       # sources of data/sources.yaml into data/raw/; see "Not all rebuildable" below
make places labels features dataset
make transfer evidence qa
make classifier # table, models, repeats and audits; several hours on 3 cores
make probmap    # about half an hour
make report     # REPORT.md, TABLES.md and README's results, from both runs
make viewer     # one self-contained page: runs/probmap/viewer/index.html
make test lint
```

`make us1985` repeats a separate check on US counties against FEMA's 1987 risk bands, `runs/m4-us-check/`, which
tests that those features join and is no part of the results above (`data/FEATURES_US.md`).

`make viewer` builds from the committed outputs alone. A clean clone's tests run without data; those that need
built tables skip.

**Not all rebuildable.** COW's data is not committed (no licence stated), so rebuilding needs its download. SIPRI's
tables are not redistributed (its terms), so `data/curated/stockpiles/delivery_mix.csv` holds rounded shares only.
`data/curated/features/bloc_air_defence_1956.csv` was made by hand from cited pages
(`scripts/bloc_air_defence_1956/README.md`). **Robots.** `make data` asks each host's robots.txt before every download and reports a refused source as not
obtained; `python -m nucprob.sources.robots` records the answer for every source in `runs/qa/robots.csv`. Each row is judged on the URL the held bytes came from: where that is not the publisher's,
`data/sources_obtained.yaml` names it, and `make data` downloads it in place of the publisher's URL. `download.geonames.org`
disallows every path, so the 37 GeoNames dumps that feed town coordinates were fetched again on 2026-11-02 from the
`DataDock/geonames` mirror on `huggingface.co` (CC BY 4.0, revision pinned), whose robots.txt allows them. All 37 have the
recorded hashes, so no table changed. The Demoscope page (its host gave no robots.txt answer) and the Pałka article are
held as Wayback captures with the recorded hashes. One held source is still from a refused path: the UK 1981 census
district totals from `www.nomisweb.co.uk` (`/api/v01/dataset/`), which feed `towns_uk_1981.csv`, the Great Britain units
of the Square Leg comparison. No allowed copy was found; whether to keep them or drop those units is the owner's decision
and is open. Earlier work also read Wikipedia through paths robots.txt disallows: `en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php` for the
military site tables of Western Europe, the UK and the US; `Special:Export` and `/w/api.php` for
`uk_districts_1981_seats.csv`; `/w/api.php` as named in the gazetteer's transcription instructions until 2026-11-02 and
for one wargame page in `data/notes/warsaw_pact.md`. Those copies are not held, the scripts and instructions now use
`/wiki/<title>?action=raw`, and the routes are listed as `past_route` rows in `runs/qa/robots.csv`. The pages those tables
cite are registered through paths robots.txt allows, with date, hash and revision, in
`data/curated/features/cited_pages.csv`. Two pages cited by those tables, on `www.armedconflicts.com`, are
refused to this project's agents and stand there with no hash. The Dutch census volume's only held copy is a landing page, since `ssh.datastations.nl` disallows the path of the PDF, and the Italian volume is not held (`held` in `data/sources.yaml`).
