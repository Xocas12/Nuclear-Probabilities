# What made a place a nuclear target? Report

Generated by `python -m nucprob.classifier.report` from `runs/classifier/` and `runs/probmap/`. Results come from
files there. Some figures describing the data come instead from the files that record it, each named where it appears:
the sizes of the sealed set (`data/curated/splits/README.md`), the transcription audit
(`data/curated/sac1956/README.md`), the West European site table
(`data/curated/features/weurope_military_sites_1965.md`), the second read of the Soviet search
(`data/notes/second_read_2026-11b.md`) and the source register (`data/sources.yaml`). List by list:
`runs/classifier/TABLES.md`.

**How to read the numbers.** The main measure is PR-AUC, the area under the precision-recall curve: rank the towns by
score and ask how many of those near the top are on the list. A model with no information scores the list's share of
positives, its **base rate**, so a PR-AUC means nothing without it: SAC's list has a base rate of 0.49, the others
together 0.07. On a list that does not claim to be complete, a town off the list is unlabeled, not a negative, so
PR-AUC there understates true precision: read such figures against each other and the base rate, not as how often a
model is right. The classifier's intervals are 95% paired bootstrap intervals; the map's (section "Probability map")
are the 5th to 95th percentiles of its draws, so 90%. "By blocks" resamples whole 300 km squares of towns; the other
interval resamples single towns. Verdicts use the unrounded bounds.

**How numbers are rounded.** Scores (PR-AUC, probabilities, shares) have two decimals. A difference between two scores
and its interval bounds have three, so that a gain and its bounds print at the same precision in README, here and in
`runs/classifier/TABLES.md`. A difference within 0.0005 of zero prints as +0.000 or -0.000: the sign gives the side of
zero on which the unrounded value lies, and verdicts use the unrounded bounds. An exact zero prints as 0.000. Where an
estimate would print equal to a bound it differs from, all three take a fourth decimal.

A comparison whose two scores never differ on its rows is shown as "constant". Where the positives lie in fewer than 5
blocks, resampling blocks measures nothing, and the block interval is shown as "n/a" with the count. In the tables,
`a_pr_auc` is the PR-AUC of the model named in the row and `b_pr_auc` that of the score it is compared against;
`by_blocks` is the block interval; `towns_without_1939` counts towns with no 1939 census figure; `cell_deg` is a map
cell's side in degrees.

**Terms.** "Document" is the general word here: most of the lists are studies, exercises, lessons and a war game, and
few are war plans (see "How much evidence there is, by side"). "Leave one document out" is the holdout
`configs/protocol.yaml` calls leave one plan out. A **scope** is the set of documents a model is fitted on: all
attacker documents, plan documents only, the pooled Warsaw Pact documents, or SAC's 1956 study alone. SAC is the US
Strategic Air Command, whose 1956 study supplies the largest list. The study groups its targets into **complexes**,
mostly a town and its surroundings, some of which hold named **subcomplexes** with their own aim points and
installations (`data/curated/sac1956/README.md`). COW is the Correlates of War project. In the `doc_type` column,
`training` is a staff-academy lesson, `wargame` a war game and `mirror` one side's guess at the other's targets. "All
permitted" means every feature the protocol allows a model to read as of a document's date: population,
administration, geography, the one industry column, transport, military sites, defences and national power
(`data/FEATURES_CLASSIFIER.md`). The **population rule** is a baseline: a logistic regression on log population. The
**state gate** is the rule deciding whether a state gets its own model. The **selection rule** decides which model is
chosen. A **sector box** (`data/curated/labels/SECTORS.yaml`) is the area a list covers: a town outside it is not a
negative of that list. The **sealed set** is the towns of the 300 km blocks in
`data/curated/splits/sealed_blocks.csv`, kept out of every fit and scored once at the end; its **blind places** are
those no committed run had scored before then (Method). **Burza** is the Polish front exercise of 1961 whose list is
`wp_1961_burza`. A **theatre share** is the stated share of an attacker's inventory assumed to be aimed at a
scenario's countries. A **folded** ROC-AUC is the larger of the ROC-AUC and one minus it, so that 0.5 means no
separation whichever way a feature points. **Pick-freeze** is the estimator of a first-order Sobol index: the share of
a quantity's variance that one input explains alone, from pairs of draws sharing that input. "Plan" alone means a
document of a type the protocol counts as a plan (war plan, target selection, requirements or policy study); "war
plan" means that type only.

## Question

Which traits of a place, as of a document's date, predict that the document designates it as a nuclear target? And
given that, what is the probability that an area is struck under a stated stockpile?

## Data

**Places.** Towns of the target country from censuses and yearbooks near the document. The floor differs by source:
10,000 for most. China's universe is the cities (*shi*) of its 1953 census, 162 towns from 26,200 people. North Korea,
North Vietnam and Mongolia have only the UN's 1956 model estimates for cities of 300,000 or more today
(`data/curated/gazetteer/README.md`), so their universes are 5, 2 and 1 towns, the smallest of 138,643, 154,016 and
122,955 people. These are the smallest towns held, not a published floor (`runs/classifier/link_audit_floors.csv`,
which counts the unsealed and sealed towns of the study). Soviet towns use the 1959 census for a June 1956 document,
because no town census exists between 1939 and 1959 (below). Western European towns use 1961 populations for documents
up to 1977: stale, not later.

**Military and defence sites of 1965.** The West European site table
(`data/curated/features/weurope_military_sites_1965.md`) had 29 rows whose fact rested on general knowledge. Each was
cited from a Wikipedia page fetched through a path robots.txt allows, corrected to what that page states, or dropped
(15 cited, 6 corrected, 8 dropped; 323 rows remain). Six fed features: the NATO air-defence Control and Reporting
Centre (CRC) at Glons is dated 1972 by its source and leaves 1965, CRC Semmerzake and the Belgian naval base at Ostend
are dropped, and Aviano is an air base, not a strike base, because no page found dates a strike role there in 1965.

**Inputs later than the document.** Removing the flagged columns does not remove all of these. The 1959 Soviet census
is in every population column. For the June 1956 document Hungary's towns are interpolated from the 1949 and 1960
censuses and the Czech lands' from 1950 and 1961 (`data/curated/gazetteer/README.md`), and Kurchatov carries a 1990
figure (`data/curated/gazetteer/closed_cities_1956.csv`). East Germany's and Poland's towns are interpolated with
end-1956 figures, half a year after the document, which also set their 10,000 floor; Slovakia's with figures of 1
January 1958; and North Korea's, North Vietnam's and Mongolia's towns are those the UN's 2018 estimates give 300,000
or more today, so which towns enter there is chosen by later size. The Western European site table runs to 1966, and
18 undated sites it holds as in role in 1965 also count for the documents of 1961 and 1964.

**Labels.** A positive is a town linked to a row the document designates (`selected = 1` in
`data/curated/labels/SCHEMA.md`). A rejected town, such as Tokyo in the Target Committee's list, is a negative. A town
named only in rows that neither designate nor reject it, such as areas "appropriate for study" or a facility in the
1963 Joint Chiefs of Staff (JCS) vulnerability table, is left out of that list. SAC targets link to the nearest town
within 10 km, or up to 75 km if the name matches closely; other lists link within 15 km. A list that concerns one
sector keeps only that sector's towns (`data/curated/labels/SECTORS.yaml`). Some boxes follow a boundary a document
states; the others are derived from the list's own linked targets plus a fixed margin, because no boundary was read,
and they say nothing about the plan's real scope. That file says which is which and gives a reason for each list read
over its whole universe. Corrected link errors are listed in `configs/protocol_amendment_6.yaml`,
`configs/protocol_amendment_7.yaml` and `configs/protocol_amendment_8.yaml`.

**Transcription.** SAC's lists, about nine positives in ten of what is trained on, were transcribed from the scan by
two independent passes of vision-language models (Claude agents), with disagreements resolved against the page
(`data/curated/sac1956/README.md`). A blind audit of a random 5% of rows, 743 lines, found 0 errors in the final
reading (95% interval 0 to 0.51%), by auditors whose own error rate was 1.3%, so the audit's reading is itself
uncertain. Of the 270 airfield rows that can be compared with a second printing, 216 lie on the low-resolution (about
200 dpi) Part II pages, and on those the confirmed misread rate was 3 in 216, well above that bound.

**Links** lose entries too: 318 of SAC's 1,215 complex entries link to no town of the universe (326 of its 873
subcomplex entries; `runs/classifier/link_audit_coverage_subcomplex.csv`), and the share varies by country: China 40
of 119, North Korea 12 of 15, North Vietnam 6 of 8, Albania 3 of 6 (`runs/classifier/link_audit_coverage.csv`). These
count entries, not towns. An entry whose cover name stands for a town and its closed city (Kapustin Yar and Znamensk,
for example) links to both, so linked complex entries give 2 more towns than their number, while linked subcomplex
entries give exactly as many towns as entries.

**These results are of the table before the nineteenth amendment.** Its Dutch towns came from a census volume fetched
through a path the host's robots.txt disallows, and its site distances were computed from cited coordinates of sites
that may be in use today. `configs/protocol_amendment_19.yaml`, committed before any refit, withdraws the Dutch towns
(and the Netherlands from the 1965 map), holds every site with no recorded end year at the centre of its 0.5-degree
cell, drops two site rows whose only source refuses this project's agents, and fixes how the refit is read. The refit
is not yet committed.

**Documents.** `data/curated/labels/PLANS.yaml` records each list's attacker, author, date, type and evidence grade.
The grades: A, a primary document or its published translation read here; B, a primary document read here that names
only part of the targets it counts, or a later classified history quoting one; C, a scholar's table or paraphrase of
an archive file not read here; D, journalism or an encyclopedia; U, a text of unrecorded origin. D and U give no
labels. The Canadian list of 1956 is U: its only source is an extract with no origin, date, page or hash, so it is not
scored. "Plan documents" below means war plans, target selections, requirements studies and policy studies at grade C
or better. Only `war_plan` documents are called war plans. SAC's 1956 list, the largest, is a requirements study, not
a war plan. Exercises, war games and lessons may enter models marked as such. Civil-defence lists and mirrors record
what a defender expected, never train a model, and are scored apart.

**How much evidence there is, by side.** The US has 1299 linked positives, of which 1292 come from one document, and
no document of type war plan. Of SAC's, 250 lie in the sealed blocks and are held out of every fit, which leaves the
1042 positives of the SAC rows in the tables below. For the USSR the registry holds **no war-plan document**: 89 rows,
88 of them from General Staff Academy lessons on one fictional scenario, giving 50 linked town-list pairs, and one
news report. Czechoslovakia has 0 linked positives and Poland 6. The registry holds 3 war-plan documents with 36 rows
between them, all Warsaw Pact, with 6 linked positives. **Only one was read at source**, the Czechoslovak plan of 1964
(grade A), and it has 0 linked positives: its rows that state nuclear weapons name units and give no place, and the
sentences that name places state no weapon. The two Polish Coastal Front plans for the Danish island of Zealand, of
1965 and 1977, are grade C, known here from a scholar's table and paraphrase of files not read. They share one author
and one universe of towns, so "independent" here means separate documents, not separate minds. One further document
with 4 rows (Coastal Front map, 1970) has a disputed status, plan or exercise, and is counted as neither. The searches
recorded in `data/notes/` found no declassified Soviet strategic target list; that does not show none exists.

**Features** (`data/FEATURES_CLASSIFIER.md`): population, administration, geography, industry (one column: today's
power plants by commissioning year), transport (rail, ports and rivers, and also airfield counts), military sites, air
and missile defences, and the target state's COW National Material Capabilities v7.0. Several are today's data
standing in for the past and are flagged. Sources, licences, dates and hashes are in `data/sources.yaml`. The pages
cited row by row in the site tables are registered apart, in `data/curated/features/cited_pages.csv`.

## Method

The analysis is fixed in `configs/protocol.yaml` and changed since only by dated amendments: four inside that file and
`configs/protocol_amendment_5.yaml` to `configs/protocol_amendment_19.yaml`. Each says what had already been read when
it was written; `configs/HISTORY.md` gives one line per amendment. **It is not blind.** Scores on SAC's spatial folds
and on the small lists, and later the full results, had been read before amendments corrected labels, links and sector
boxes. Burza's sector box was drawn after its effect on Burza's scores had been seen. The rule that a block interval
needs positives in 5 blocks was set after the row it changes had been seen (7 positives in 2 blocks, verdict "neither"
before and after; `configs/protocol_amendment_9.yaml`).

The one blind test uses the **sealed set**: the 467 places of the sealed blocks, held out of every fit. An early check
(two test models run before this classifier) had scored 194 of them before the blocks were drawn
(`data/curated/splits/README.md`). Of the other 273, committed outputs of earlier runs held scores for 123 more, which
the drawing of the set missed (`data/curated/splits/scored_in_history.csv`). Of the remaining 150, the 149 with a SAC
label are the **blind places**. Their labels are in the committed label files, so the seal is a procedure and not a
secret. It has been scored once; see below.

- **Models.** Baselines: logistic regression on log population, and the same plus one intercept per country.
  Candidates: L2-penalised (ridge) logistic regression and LightGBM (gradient-boosted trees) with fixed settings.
  Nothing was tuned.
- **Positive-unlabeled.** On a list that does not claim to be complete, a town off it is unlabeled, not a negative.
  Each of 50 fits (**bags**) keeps all positives and draws as many unlabeled towns as the list has positives.
- **Holdouts.** Leave one document out (a document's lists and towns leave together); spatial blocks of 300 km in 5
  folds on three shifted grids; temporal, in both directions around 1956, splitting documents by date only, so a town
  listed in both periods is in both the fit and the scored rows (`configs/protocol.yaml`, holdouts). Spatial folds
  have no buffer: on the first grid 15% of held-out rows have a training town within 25 km and 36% within 50 km
  (`runs/classifier/block_neighbours.csv`).
- **Reading.** Mean per-list PR-AUC, weighted by positives, on lists with 2 or more. Neither interval includes the
  variance of refitting a model.
- **Fit checks.** The document identification classifier (Results, "What the gains rest on") diverged, so an amendment
  fixed before it was run (`configs/protocol_amendment_15.yaml`) refitted the primary models to see whether they do
  too: every bag of the four models on each scope's whole rows, the five folds of the first spatial grid, each
  left-out document and both temporal directions, 8,832 bag fits in all. A test checks that the refitting code draws
  and fits bags exactly as the primary run's code does. Of the 8,832, 0 were called diverged. A LightGBM fit is called
  diverged if its training log loss ends more than 0.01 above its lowest value or a raw score passes 50; a logistic
  fit if its solver stops at its iteration cap or a decision value passes 50. LightGBM's training loss never ended
  above its lowest value, and its largest raw score was 8.8; no logistic solver stopped at its cap of 5,000
  iterations, and the largest decision value was 25.6 (`runs/classifier/divergence/summary.csv`). This shows whether
  the fits converged, not that the models are right. The grids shifted by 100 and 200 km and the fits that keep a
  left-out document's towns were not refitted.
- **Which model.** Each scope's candidate was chosen on spatial blocks alone, and the spatial differences below are
  read on the same rows, so they are optimistic by the choice between two candidates. The left-out and temporal rows
  are not read where the model was chosen. The protocol's "nothing is chosen on a held-out set" concerns model
  settings.

## Probability map

The map answers: if a stated number of warheads were delivered, and each fell on a town in proportion to the score of
a model of these lists, how likely is it that an area sees at least one? It is a consequence of stated assumptions,
not a forecast of any war. The viewer is `runs/probmap/viewer/index.html`, built by `make viewer`.

**Stockpile and allocation.** `data/curated/stockpiles/` holds cited inventories by state, year and, where a source
splits them, delivery, with cited or expressly stated assumptions on availability, reliability and loss to defences.
`configs/scenarios.yaml` states who is assumed to fire on whom and what share of the inventory goes there. Those are
assumptions, not findings. **The theatre shares have no source**; these are all of them, as (low, mode, high) of a
triangular draw:

| scenario | attackers | low | mode | high |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| us_1956_on_bloc | US | 0.75 | 0.90 | 1.00 |
| us_1950_stock_on_bloc | US | 0.75 | 0.90 | 1.00 |
| ussr_1965_on_west | USSR | 0.25 | 0.50 | 1.00 |
| modern_us_russia | US, France, UK, Russia | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 |
| modern_us_russia_half | US, France, UK, Russia | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 |
| modern_us_china | US | 0.10 | 0.25 | 0.50 |
| modern_us_china | China | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 |
| modern_india_pakistan | India, Pakistan | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 |

Every historical scenario takes its central reliability (0.65, from the abort, operational-loss and ineffective-bomb
fractions) and its range of losses to defences (0.07 for mid-1953 to 0.27 for mid-1955) from one source's estimates
for Soviet bombers attacking the US (Foreign Relations of the United States, FRUS, 1952-54, vol. II part 1, document
66); the reliability's spread of plus or minus 0.07 is this study's assumption, not the source's. These stand in for
SAC's bombers in 1956 and Soviet missiles and bombers in 1965, because no source for those forces was found. A
delivered warhead falls on a town with probability equal to the town's share of the scenario's total score, and an
area's probability is 1 - (1 - share)^warheads. Nothing is optimised, and no count of weapons by area is computed or
written. One interceptor force is drawn once and shared among the attackers it faces. The modern universe is Natural
Earth's populated places: a map-label set of 7,342 places, not a census, thinner in some countries than others, of
which 6,091 are kept: towns with 10,000 or more people, without France's territories outside Europe
(`runs/probmap/modern_universe.csv`). The 1965 scenario's towns carry populations of about 1961.

**No United Kingdom in 1965.** The only UK table held, Great Britain's 1981 districts, came through a path of
`www.nomisweb.co.uk` that robots.txt disallows, and was withdrawn (`configs/protocol_amendment_10.yaml`). So the 1965
map covers Denmark, the FRG, the Netherlands, Belgium and Italy, and its unsourced theatre share falls on them alone,
so its continental cells read higher than they would if the UK were among the targets. Warheads are shared out per
row, so how a country's towns are cut into rows also moves the map: the rows are municipalities, and how much that
matters was not measured.

**Score.** The selection rule in `configs/protocol.yaml` selected **the population rule**, by highest PR-AUC on a
left-out document (0.71). The candidates ("portable" in the table) use only features that exist in every era without
any present-day facility data: population, its rank within the country, population within 25, 50 and 100 km, distance
to a town of 100,000, distance to the capital, distance to a coast, and whether it is the national capital or a
regional centre (ten features). So the map's score holds no defence, military or national-power term. It is fitted on
all attacker documents, exercises included, and is not one state's model, because no state passes the gate with enough
outside its largest document.

The population rule won, so **the map's shares are read from town population and nothing else**. What a town produced,
housed or defended plays no part. The selection rule chose it by PR-AUC, which depends only on the order of towns: on
a left-out document nothing richer has been shown to order them better. The shares use the logistic's fitted values,
and how far those values move with population was not tested by that choice.

| model | left_out_document_pr_auc | left_out_document_without_sac | spatial_pr_auc | selected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| population rule | 0.71 | 0.22 | 0.71 | True |
| portable logistic | 0.67 | 0.19 | 0.68 | False |
| portable LightGBM | 0.65 | 0.19 | 0.72 | False |

**What that score does.** On the 1956 towns population runs over a 620-fold range and the median score over a 6.8-fold
range, and a town's share of warheads is its share of the score. So a large town gets only a few times the warheads of
a small one, and a cell's probability depends on how many towns it holds as well as on how many people live there.
Across 1956 cells the probability correlates 0.52 with the count of towns and 0.94 with log people. In 5 of 7
scenarios it correlates more with log people than with the count of towns; in `us_1950_stock_on_bloc` and
`modern_india_pakistan` it correlates more with the count of towns (`runs/probmap/score_spread.csv`).

**Scenarios.** Intervals are the 5th to 95th percentile over 200 joint draws of model (block resample, one bag) and
stockpile. They hold fixed the allocation rule, who fires on whom and the list of towns, so they are narrower than
what is actually unknown. "Mean width" is the mean over cells of that interval; the two columns before it hold the
stockpile at its median, then the model at its median. Cells over one half are counted within each draw. Cells
saturate because warheads can outnumber cells while not outnumbering towns: `ussr_1965_on_west` has 1050 median
delivered warheads, 1843 towns and 355 cells, and a cell of several towns passes one half before each of its towns
would. Where most cells are over one half the map says little beyond that. Sealed towns are not scored, so a 1956 cell
holding only sealed towns is absent from the map. In all 7 scenarios the mean width with the model held at its median
is larger than with the stockpile held at its median: the stockpile draws, not the model, make most of the width.

"Delivered" in the tables below is before one more step: towns of the sealed set are not scored and draw no warheads,
so the 1956 map cuts what is delivered by the share of people living in scored towns, 0.82. That is a population
share, not the share of score the sealed towns would hold: they have no score, and the population share is fixed
before any model draw. In `us_1956_on_bloc`, of 1380 delivered at the median, 1130 fall on scored towns. Part of the
spread of delivered warheads comes from cited inventories and part from the unsourced theatre shares. The theatre
share alone accounts for 9% (India, `modern_india_pakistan`) to 96% (France, `modern_us_russia`) of the variance of an
attacker's delivered warheads: a first-order Sobol index by the pick-freeze estimator on 20,000 draws, with standard
errors up to 0.007 (`runs/probmap/share_variance.csv`).

Counts of warheads and cells are whole below 100 and to the nearest ten from 100, since the theatre shares behind them
have no source. In the second table each figure is the median with the 5th to 95th percentile; `on_scored_towns` is
"all" where the sealed set holds nobody; `delivered_shares_at_mode` is the interval with every share held at its mode,
from separate draws. Holding a share at its mode also moves the centre, so that column is not a split of the
interval's width.

**By delivery.** Warheads available by state and delivery after the stated assumptions, and those delivered. The
shares come from SIPRI and the Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC; `data/curated/stockpiles/delivery_mix.csv`),
on bases that differ by state. For 1950 NRDC has all on bombers, 330 loaded, more than the 299 Our World in Data

measured on strategic or deployed forces is applied to the whole inventory range, so a Soviet row of 1965 spreads
NRDC's strategic shares over a stockpile that also holds tactical weapons with no known split. Reliability has one
range for every modern delivery, because no source separates them. Each delivery draws from that range separately, so
the split leaves the expected delivered total unchanged and changes only how much the draws average out. These are
totals for a whole force. No delivery is assigned to any area. The table by state and delivery is in
`runs/classifier/TABLES.md` ("Probability map: warheads available and delivered").

**Domain shift.** A classifier told the 3,686 training towns from the 2,276 modern towns in the countries the modern
scenarios target (of the 6,091 kept worldwide) at ROC-AUC 0.97, where 0.5 would mean no shift. For scale, it told
SAC's towns from the other training towns at 0.89. 18% of modern towns lie outside the training's 1st-99th percentile
on `log_pop`, the worst feature. There are no modern labels, so performance on modern towns cannot be measured at all.
Every modern row is an **extrapolation**: it applies lists of 1945-1977 to towns of today, on a selective set of named
places. The ROC-AUC is measured on all 10 portable features, of which the map's score uses one, `log_pop`, the worst
feature named above. Feature by feature: on its own, `log_pop` separates the two sets at a folded ROC-AUC of 0.87 (0.5
is no separation); the single features run from 0.50 (`pop_rank_pct`) to 0.87 (`log_pop`), so the one feature the
score reads is the one that separates the sets most. With one feature left out, the classifier's ROC-AUC stays between
0.92 (without `log_pop`) and 0.97: leaving out `log_pop` lowers it by 0.05, the most of any feature. That measures the
shift; it does not show the score valid there.

Every feature's three figures are in `runs/classifier/TABLES.md` ("Probability map: domain shift").

## What the evidence cannot support

- **Any statement about Soviet war plans.** The USSR is refused its own model: 0 independent plans, needs 2; 0 linked
  positives, needs 50; 0 positives outside its largest plan, needs 15. The Soviet rows that are used come from General
  Staff Academy lessons and Warsaw Pact exercises, and say what those taught or rehearsed.
- **That SAC's list is free of error.** See how it was transcribed, under Data, and `data/ISSUES.md` for what has been
  corrected.
- **A state's own model.** Each state is refused. A model of Warsaw Pact war plans in particular has no basis: 6
  linked positives, none from a plan read at source. The pooled model is exercise-based.
- **Prediction for a plan not in the data.** See the main result. The map's score carries this limit into every cell.
- **A blind gain over population plus country.** On the only blind places, 149 towns of SAC's list, no selected model
  beat it by both intervals (Results, "The sealed set on blind places").
- **A pooled gain as more than each document's base rate.** The columns name a row's document 98.1% of the time
  against 44.4% for naming the largest, so a model fitted on pooled lists can learn which document a row belongs to
  (Results, "What the gains rest on").
- **Causes.** These are associations between a town's traits and its presence on a list. No design here separates one
  trait's effect from another's or from the planners' reasons.
- **What planners knew.** Features record what existed according to later sources.
- **Any statement about a present-day plan.** Modern map rows extrapolate from lists of 1945 to 1977.
