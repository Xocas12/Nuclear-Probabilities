Targets of 1956

Towns of the Soviet bloc with 10,000 or more people near 1956, against the complexes of SAC's 1956 target list. Colours show how likely a model trained on every feature family (population, administration, geography, industry, military, transport) thought each town was a target.

Legend

How well each model ranks

Spatial cross-validation in 300 km blocks (mean over 15 folds, ± spread); the random split alongside shows how little nearby towns leak. Precision@k: share of true targets among the k towns ranked highest, k = the number of targets.

What each family adds

PR-AUC of LightGBM with the population family plus one other family, and with every family but one. "No anachronisms" drops the features that use later knowledge (the defence-industry guide, the secret nuclear complex, today's airfields, railways and ports).

By country

Out-of-fold PR-AUC within each country, for countries with at least ten towns on and off the list. Share: the country's share of targets.

About this check

The numbers check that every feature family joins end to end; they are not findings, since the protocol is frozen only at milestone M5. The features describe each town as of June 1956; the catalogue (data/FEATURES.md) gives each one's sources and flags the anachronisms.

A town counts as on the list when a complex or sub-complex of the SAC 1956 study (Part I, transcribed in full) lies within 10 km of it, or matches its name a little further off. Predictions are out of fold: each town was scored by a model that never saw it.

A fifth of the towns form a sealed test set, scored once at the end of the project; they carry no prediction here. A few towns have no label because their entries may be on a page missing from the scanned study (Astrakhan, Ashgabat, Arzamas among them).

Sources: SAC study SM 129-56 (National Security Archive, EBB 538); pop-stat.mashke.org and Demoscope (censuses); GeoNames (coordinates); Dexter and Rodionov's guide to the Soviet defence industry; WRI power plants; OurAirports; Natural Earth; CShapes 2.0; AWS terrain tiles.
